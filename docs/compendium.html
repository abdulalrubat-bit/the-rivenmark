Book I

The Premise

What broke, what it cost, and why anyone goes down there.

The Aegis is shattered. Raw magic kills now unless it runs through Spirit-wrought Steel. The Vanguard descend to cut Arcane Slag from the Hollow-Thralls, find a ley-gate, and hold it open long enough to carry the haul out from under the Shroud of Dúath.

That is the whole of the stated fiction, and everything else in the game is built on top of it. The pieces below are the load-bearing ones — each is named somewhere in the game and each is deliberately under-explained.

The Aegis
Whatever it was, it held magic away from people. Its shattering is the founding event and the game's title is a reference to it — the Riven mark. Nothing yet says who made it, when it broke, or whether it can be remade.
Raw magic
Lethal on contact. The only safe conduit is Spirit-wrought Steel — which is also the second-best gear rarity, so the material that keeps a Vanguard alive is the same material their kit is graded against.
Arcane Slag
Cut from the dead. It is simultaneously the extraction quota, the experience currency, and the thing the realm is being strip-mined for. Every body is worth a fixed number of it — a thrall one, the Deceiver thirty.
Ley-gates
The way out. They do not open on demand: the gate answers only once the quota is met, and answering it summons the Deceiver to it. Getting out means going through him.
The Shroud of Dúath
What lies over the delves. Dúath also names a threshold (delve 19) and an amulet in the Regalia (Pendant of Dúath), so it is a place, a boundary and a relic at once.
The hive-mind
What claims the dead. Thralls are described as tethered to it; Zayd's whole gift is severing those tethers. On a death the game says plainly: “The hive-mind claimed another of the Clear-Sighted.”
The Clear-Sighted
An order or a condition — the game only ever says the Vanguard are “two of the Clear-Sighted” and that they fight unhelmeted. That is why there is no head slot in the whole gear system.
The void-brand
Every enemy carries an asymmetrical violet mark. It is stated to be the tell that these were people who gave up their will, rather than monsters. It rides on whichever part of the body moves least.
“Cut, gather, and find the ley-gate.” The first delve, and the whole game in six words
Book II

The Realm

Five sundered regions. Each delve is cut from one of them and takes its stone, its earth and its lamplight.

A region is not a level — it is an environment table a level draws from. The first delve deliberately rolls all five, because its job is to exercise every environment the generator can build. Deeper rungs widen their pool as they go, so a shallow delve is one place and the deepest could be cut from anywhere in the realm.

Each region also carries a layout grammar: how large its rooms run, how wide its corridors are, and how many chambers get cut. Kraggen-Tor is tight and cramped; the Dead Firth is wide and open.

The Slag-Moors of Drak-Hald
The Weeping Keep of Tor-Varden

Dead ash. Rooms 6–13, corridors 3 wide, 7 chambers. Amber lamplight over brown earth — the default face of the realm.

The Rending Gorges of Vaelk
The Kael-Dorm Redoubt

Split earth. The largest rooms in the realm (7–16) and the fewest chambers — cold blue stone, azure lamps, wide and quiet.

Kraggen-Tor, the Iron-Teeth
The Shatter-Gate of Ghor

Volcanic west. The tightest layout in the game — rooms 5–11, narrow corridors. Red rock under a hot orange lamp.

The Rot-Weald
The Heart-Rot Clearing

Creeping mutation in the southern forest. Nine chambers — the most subdivided region. Green earth, sickly moss, pale green light.

The Dead Firth
The Ash-Shoals

A blackened ocean, drained and refilled with drifting ash. The widest rooms (8–17) and eleven chambers — grey on grey under a cold white lamp.

Book III

The Vanguard

Two of the Clear-Sighted, fighting unhelmeted. Each is immune to a different one of the Deceiver’s tricks.

Both heroes channel through Spirit-wrought Steel: they strike, and the magic leaves the edge as a crescent. range is how far that crescent carries and how far the blade picks a target; sweep is its half-width, so a wider sweep mows a broader swathe rather than punching through one more body.

Isaac, the Unyielding Shield

The vanguard’s anchor. Sun-Gold magic, a sun-emblazoned shield, and no purchase for the Deceiver’s illusions. He is slower and hits less often, but he is tougher and his crescents cut far wider — a sweep of 33 against Zayd’s 21. He is the crowd answer.

Zayd, the Piercing Truth

A tactician of Kael. The Sapphire Glaive severs hive-mind tethers, and inverted ley-lines hold no confusion for him. Fast, long-reaching, narrow, and fragile — 92 life is the thinnest margin in the game. He is the precision answer.

The design tell

Isaac is proof against illusion; Zayd is proof against the hive-mind and against inverted ley-lines. The Deceiver’s two signature tricks are illusion (mirages, splitting) and the tether. Each hero is written as the counter to one half of the antagonist — which always implied the two of them were meant to descend together.

They do now. Both go down, and you hold one at a time — see Book IV. The one you are not holding waits with his own wounds, and the Deceiver’s last phase cannot be answered by either of them alone.

Strike forms

Three shapes cycle in order: the blade comes across level (slash), then up from low (rise), then down the centre (chop). The chop pulls the hero furthest into the blow. The forms are drawing only — damage, reach and the crescent’s hit geometry are identical between them, so no strike is better than another.

Rank

Slag is experience. It drives a hero level that persists across delves, to a maximum of 60. Each level is worth +6 life, +1.15 damage and a sliver of ward (capped at 12%). The curve is deliberately shallow early and steep late: 38 × level1.62 slag to reach a given rank, so the first delves move and the fiftieth still means something.

Power level is the single number the ladder is measured against: it averages what the hero brings with what the kit brings, so a rank-40 hero in rags and a rank-10 hero in a full set both read honestly. A delve tells you plainly whether you are under it — well within you, an even match, above your weight, or far beyond you. It is advice, never a lock.

Book IV

The Kit

Nine abilities across two heroes, and a beat slow enough that you have to choose which one.

The blade is always there and always free. Everything in this book sits on top of it, on a shared global cooldown of 1.2 seconds — press one thing and the rest of the kit greys for a beat. That single number is what makes the kit a sequence of decisions rather than five buttons to mash.

Two exceptions, and both are deliberate. Focal Decryption is off the beat, because an interrupt that has to wait its turn is not an interrupt. And the swap costs a beat and a quarter — 1.5 seconds — because changing who is standing there should never be free.

The beat, and what it feels like

A heavy landing freezes the simulation for up to 90ms — not the animation, the whole world — which is the cheapest way a 2D canvas can make a blow feel like it had mass. Damage numbers are ranked rather than uniform: a scratch is small and dim, a heavy blow is large, bright and rimmed in its own colour, a soaked one shrinks and greys, and a heal is green. The camera shake is bound to the share of your life a hit took, not to a constant, so a graze and a blow that nearly ended the run do not shake the same.

Isaac — Sun-Gold Charges

A combo resource, maximum 3. One builder makes them, two spenders eat all three. He is the hero who stands still on purpose.

The blade, which pays for both

Neither pool is filled by the bar any more. A swing that lands builds it — 1 Charge for Isaac, 14 Tension for Zayd — once per press, however many crescents that press threw. Swinging at empty floor builds nothing. The attack you have to ask for is what pays for the attack you choose the moment of.

A blow gathered to 55% or better also goes through a raised guard. That is the only answer to a braced body: a wind-up costs a second of being easy to reach and makes the loudest noise on the Clamour, which is the price of walking into something rather than round it.

Zayd — Ley-Tension

A pool of 100 that regenerates at 4 a second and refills completely on a successful interrupt. Interrupting is not a utility he can afford occasionally; it is how he is paid.

The swap

Both of the Clear-Sighted go down. You hold one; the other waits. Tapping the swap banks everything the current hero is carrying — life, Charges or Tension, jars, every cooldown mid-count — and takes up whatever the other one was left with. Wounds are kept. Swapping is not a heal; a Zayd you left at a third of his life is a Zayd you come back to at a third of his life. A new delve is a new delve, and both start whole.

It costs 1.5 seconds of beat and puts itself on a 5-second cooldown, and the frame changes with the hero: Isaac’s heavy bronze and Sun-Gold, Zayd’s narrow Azure geometry.

The gear question, and the answer taken

Two heroes and one set of Regalia is a decision that had to go one way or the other. The kit is shared. Both wear the same pieces, and an affix forged for one hero still pays out for whoever is holding it — a Sun-Wide roll gives Isaac more sweep than it gives Zayd, because it is scaling each of them from their own numbers.

The alternative — two independent loadouts — doubles every drop’s value and halves how often any single drop matters, and turns the Forge into two Forges. This is the reversible choice, and it is written down here so that reversing it is a decision rather than a discovery.

Book V

The Bestiary

Eight archetypes walk the horde. Each asks a different question, and every one of them wears the void-brand.

A role decides how a body fights, not how hard. Before roles existed, five enemy types read as one type at five sizes; now each is a different problem with a different answer.

Champions

Roughly one pack in six is led by a branded champion. A champion is bodily bigger (+22%), carries 2.6× the life and 1.35× the damage, moves slightly slower, notices you from further off, and is worth at least 4× the slag. Champions are also one of only three sources of Regalia in the entire game.

The ceiling, and why it counts feet rather than bodies

No more than 46 of them are ever on their feet at once. The obvious reading — cap how many are placed — breaks extraction outright: a delve carries about 1.4× its own quota in slag and the quota runs to 270, at roughly a slag a thrall, so a capped map would stop containing enough to open its own gate.

Capping the awake instead gives the same wall in front of you and leaves the delve full behind it. A body over the ceiling stays down until something ahead of it falls — but anything you actually hit gets up regardless, so nothing on the map can become unkillable and no slag is ever locked away behind the cap.

Book VI

The Deceiver

Fifty-two rungs cannot each have a hand-built boss, and fifty-two copies of one boss is worse than having none.

The avatar at the end of a delve is always the Deceiver — he is the fiction. But what he is capable of is rolled per rung and stays rolled, so the same rung always answers with the same thing. A delve is learnable, and two rungs apart are not the same fight.

He is drawn to the gate by the slag counter filling, not summoned by you. He blinks rather than walks. He summons lieutenants, he splits into mirages, and he takes his name from what he can do.

The seven epithets

A rung rolls one to three of these, by depth. The boss’s name is assembled from them — The Deceiver, the Sundering and the Legion — and the first one is stated plainly on the delve card before you descend, not shouted at you when he arrives. An unmutated boss is simply The Gilded Deceiver.

The fight hands you the means

Those five seconds have exactly one answer and it is Zayd’s. But the escort usually goes down on Isaac — he is who survives the cleave — which leaves Zayd’s Ley-Tension banked and frozen since the last swap, possibly at nothing, and the swap itself possibly four seconds into its own five.

A wipe you could not have answered because of a cooldown is a coin toss, not a mechanic. So the moment the breath begins the swap comes off cooldown and the Spellbreaker arrives full. The phase asks only whether you use it.

Burning him down raw inside those five seconds is a real alternative and stays one on purpose — but his life scales with the rung and the window does not, so it stops being one as you go deeper.

The Uninvited

The avatar at the gate is scheduled — you know he is coming because you can watch the slag counter fill. The invader is not. Somewhere past the 34-second mark he simply arrives, and the delve says: “Something has come for you. He was not called. Kill him, or lose him in the dark.”

He is lighter than the gate avatar — he is a fight you did not plan for and may not be equipped for, so running is a real answer. Keep more than 380 units off him for 78 seconds and he stops looking: “He has lost you. For now.” He leaves no loot and no kill.

But he hunts alone, he is faster than the scheduled one, the ground does not close behind him, and if you put him down he always drops a piece of the Regalia. Being hunted through a delve you did not plan to fight in is the price, and it should be paid.

Book VII

The Delve

Fifty-two rungs, cut fresh every descent, populated at generation and cleared by walking into it.

A delve is not a wave arena. It is a place that is already full when you arrive: packs sit dormant until something wakes them, so pressure comes from what you walk into rather than from what is pushed at you.

How the ground is made

Cellular automata gives organic rock. A carved skeleton of chambers and corridors is cut through it afterwards to give maze structure and guarantee the place is traversable. A flood fill from the spawn then fills in anything it could not reach, so every open cell in the finished map is reachable from every other one. The world is 2000 × 2000 units on a 50 × 50 grid.

Rooms you can name

A braided maze is uniform corridor and near-uniform chamber — nothing you would recognise on the way back. Three to five existing rooms get dressed with a shape instead:

  • Hall — pillars on a regular grid. The one shape that reads as built rather than eroded, and it gives a fight cover to break line of sight on.
  • Barracks — stub walls in rows, like bunk partitions off one side.
  • Collapse — a caved-in ceiling: a scatter of loose blocks, no pattern at all.
  • Cistern — left deliberately bare. A wide empty room is its own kind of place.

Chasms are cut afterwards — ground that cannot be stood on rather than rock you walk round.

The landmark at the gate

The ley-gate never stands in open cave. Each region builds its own landmark around it, laid square as masonry rather than stepped round a circle, so the place you extract from reads as something someone built:

Things in the rooms

Pitch barrels
Break for 62 across 96 units, but with a 1.1s fuse — long enough to walk out of. The blade aims itself, so the fuse is the player’s whole agency here. Pitch spreads: a barrel takes its neighbours with it.
Pillars
Come down for 88 across 78 units after only 0.5s — a column of stone does not hesitate long, but not nothing. They leave rubble.
Scenery
Bones, spilled coin, horned skulls, tombs, crates, urns, banners and chains. Some of it is standing cover; most of it is dressing that says what the room was.

Coffers

Everything in the delve up to now came off a body, which makes the whole map one long corridor between fights. A coffer in a dead end is a reason to take the turning you did not have to take. They sit in penned-in ground by preference — nooks, ends, the backs of rooms — and opening one is walking onto it, because there is no free hand on a phone for an action button.

The ramp

The first eight rungs teach the game by leaving things out of it, not by explaining them. Each adds exactly one thing the player has to have an answer for, and until they have met it nothing else is allowed to arrive at the same time. Nothing here is announced — the player is not told a lesson is happening, only handed one thing at a time until they are not being handed things any more.

The first delve carries no champion, no pitch, no invader, no epithet on the avatar, and no escort holding him. From the ninth rung the delve is the whole game: the roster does not grow again, and what changes is how much of it turns up.

The ladder

Across the fifty-two rungs the expected power climbs from 4 to 100 and the slag quota from 125 to 270. The region pool widens with depth, champions grow more common, and the packs themselves shift composition — heavies only appear in the outer bands of a delve, so the far end of a map is a different fight from the near end.

Book VIII

The Gate

Meeting the quota does not open the ley-gate. It draws him to it.

A delve is bounded at roughly two minutes — a player clears bodies as fast as a populated map can present them. Everything that makes a run long happens at the gate.

The sequence

  • Fill the quota. Slag is cut from bodies. The counter is visible the whole time, so you always know how close he is.
  • He arrives. Reaching the quota summons the Deceiver to the gate, with his lieutenants around him.
  • Put him down. Only then does the gate answer.
  • Wind it open. Four seconds standing inside it. Step out and it winds back down.
  • Leave, or hold. “Step through, or hold it and let them come.”

The hold

Holding the gate is the delve’s second act, and the only part of a run that can be stretched. It runs in surges rather than as one smooth ramp — each is a build, a crest and a short lull, and the lull is the window where leaving is a real choice rather than a scramble. Thirty seconds a surge, six of them quiet.

Bodies the gate calls are harder than bodies the delve was cut with, and each surge harder than the last — more life, more damage, and increasingly likely to be a champion. Payment lands at the crest of each surge you outlast, climbing steeply, with a piece of gear every other surge.

Why the hold ends at ten

Ten surges is five minutes; with a two-minute delve in front of it, that is a session and a ceiling the economy can be balanced against. It is also the only thing stopping the hold paying for itself — an unbounded hold hands you gear faster than it can threaten you. Measured, one did: twenty-eight surges, no deaths, a hundred and sixty times a delve’s own coin. On the tenth the gate shuts and takes you with it. Being thrown out of a delve you survived is an extraction, not a death.

The corpse run

Dying is not a reset. Everything unbanked — the bag and the run’s coin — stays in the delve, and the delve keeps it. Descend into the same level again and it is lying where you fell.

Only one corpse exists at a time, so dying on the way back to a corpse costs you the first one. That is the whole tension, and it is why the walk back is worth anything. Because the delve is cut fresh every descent, the recorded point is a hint rather than an address — the corpse is snapped onto ground that exists this time.

Reclaiming it is an ambush. The delve raises a ring of three to seven champions around you and forces the ley-gate open: you get your things back and a way out at the same moment, and no time at all to spend deciding which. “It wants them back. The ley-gate is open — go.”

What is worn survives either way. Losing the kit off your back to one bad tap would make every delve a decision about whether to risk playing at all.

Book IX

The Regalia

Eight slots, no helm — because the Clear-Sighted fight unhelmeted, and the compendium is explicit about it.

Power comes from the rank you have earned and the gear you are wearing, and both survive the delve that won them. Nothing is accumulated by mutating the hero — every stat is recomputed from base whenever anything changes, because gear comes off as easily as it goes on.

The names are doing fictional work

The blade is an Aegis-Shard — a piece of the thing that broke. The mail and the rings belong to a Vigil nothing else in the game names. The boots are of the Last March. The girdle holds light, which is exactly what the Shaman takes. Read together, the Regalia is the kit of a specific failed defence, scattered across the realm and now worn by the people still going down there.

Book X

The Gate-House

Where the Vanguard come back to. Four stations, a vendor, and somewhere for the money to go.

Coin is the only thing in the delve that is not luck: whatever the drops do, coin accumulates. That is what makes it the right currency for buying your way past a bad streak.

The vendor

  • Commission a piece — a Spirit-wrought piece for a slot you have never filled. Three affixes, rolled at depth.
  • Temper a piece — reroll every affix on something you are wearing, keeping its grade.
  • Open a reliquary — a piece of the Sundered Regalia. It costs less the longer one has refused to drop.

That last is bad-luck protection, stated as fiction: every delve that ends without a Regalia piece pushes a counter along and the reliquary gets cheaper, to a floor. Finding one resets it.

The four stations

Everything at the vendor is a consumable, so a good run bought a slightly better next run and nothing else — there was nowhere for money to accumulate into. The Hall is that place. Three tiers each, every tier paid for once and kept forever, and each one changes a number the player can already feel.

What comes home

Gear outlives a delve, but only what you carried out. Extract and the bag comes home; die and it stays on the floor where you fell. The bag holds twenty pieces, the vault sixty before the Hall widens it, and a corpse will hold on to ten of them for you.

Book XI

Open Ground

Named in the game, unexplained by it. The most useful pages in the book, because nothing is standing on them yet.

Everything below is currently a proper noun with no story behind it. The game will not contradict you, whatever you decide.

The Aegis, and who held it

The founding event is a shattering, and the game never says whose it was. The best blade in the game is a shard of it — so pieces are recoverable, which implies it could in principle be reassembled. That is a whole campaign sitting unclaimed.

The Vigil

Three of the eight Regalia pieces belong to it — the mail and both rings. It is the only organisation in the game named exclusively through loot. Who were they, what were they keeping vigil over, and is the Regalia scattered because they lost?

Dúath

A shroud, a threshold at the nineteenth delve, and a pendant. Place, boundary and relic. Nothing says which came first.

The Clear-Sighted

Isaac and Zayd are “two of” them, so there are more. They fight unhelmeted — is that a discipline, a vow, or a requirement of the sight itself? The hive-mind “claims” them on death, which suggests the Clear-Sighted are exactly what it wants.

The Hearth-Wardens and the Frost-Scholars of Kael

Two orders, two schools of magic (Sun-Gold and Azure), and a city or house called Kael that also names a redoubt in Vaelk. Two named traditions and one named place, with no relationship stated between them.

Why the Deceiver is Gilded

He is gold; the Regalia is gold; the coin is gold; the rarest rolled grade is gold. The game colour-codes wealth and the antagonist identically and never comments on it. That is either a rich seam or a coincidence to make deliberate.

The void-brand

Stated to mark people who gave up their will. So every body in the delve consented to something. Nobody has written what was offered.

The four empty names

Bonewright Yard — someone worked bone there, as a trade. The Silent Choir — the cantors are named for singing and there is a place named for singers who stopped. Hollow Processional — thralls are Hollow, and a processional is a route walked in ceremony. Drowned Barrows — graves under water, in a realm with a drained ocean.

What the Shaman is burning

It carries a caged, glowing heart on its staff and its two spells are fire and the theft of light. It has no melee at all. Whatever is in that cage is the source of both, and it is the only enemy in the game that is visibly carrying its power rather than being it.

Where the gate goes

A ley-gate is the way out and it must be held open against the delve. Held too long, it shuts and takes you with it — and that counts as escaping. Nothing says what is on the other side, or who is holding it from that end.
